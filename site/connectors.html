Signed webhook

Each verified tap sends this to your address:

POST your-endpoint
{"type":"tap.verified","timestamp":"2026-10-06T21:14:03.120Z",
 "data":{"id":"042D6B02936980:61","knot":"desk-candle","uid":"042D6B02936980",
         "counter":61,"tappedAt":"2026-10-06T21:14:03.120Z","action":"desk-webhook"}}

The headers follow Standard Webhooks: webhook-id (the same on every retry), webhook-timestamp and webhook-signature, an HMAC-SHA256 of the id, timestamp and body. Your receiver should check the signature, refuse old timestamps and ignore ids it has already seen.

Because the format is standard, a signed webhook can also reach services that don't have their own connector, through your own small receiver.

Matter bridge

The bridge runs next to knurd as a small Node.js program and shows each tag as a contact sensor. A tap "opens" the sensor for a few seconds, and a routine in your smart-home app does the rest: "when Desk candle opens, turn on the lamp".

  • Contact sensors work in every major app, so one bridge covers Apple Home, Google Home, Alexa and SmartThings.
  • The bridge only has its own sensors. It can't see or control any other device in your home.
  • It only accepts signed taps from knurd on the same computer, and a burst of taps counts as one.
  • It isn't Matter-certified yet, so apps call it an "uncertified accessory".
